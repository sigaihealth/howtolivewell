A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR LIFE IN THE U.S.

Where to look for food, housing, and care support

When a basic need cannot wait, start with a service that can connect you to local or official help. Availability and eligibility vary, so use the linked sources to check the program and location that apply to you.

This page offers general information. Services and rules can vary by state and change over time. Check the sources and ask a qualified professional about your own medical, legal, or financial situation.

11 STEPS

Steps you can take

11 steps to explore. Choose one that fits your situation; each step has a source you can check.

01

Use 211 to find local help

#

A local specialist can point you toward food, housing, utility, and other services.

First step

Call 211 or search your local 211 site. Ask what is available where you live.

Source

  • United Way 211: Local help
02

Check food benefit options

#

SNAP may help eligible households buy food; rules and applications vary by state.

First step

Use USDA’s state directory to reach your state SNAP office and check current eligibility.

Source

  • USDA: SNAP state directory
03

Find care even without insurance

#

Community health centers serve people with or without insurance and adjust charges by income.

First step

Search by ZIP code on HRSA’s health-center finder; call the center to ask about costs and appointments.

Sources

  • HRSA: Find a health center
  • HRSA: Health center cost and eligibility
04

Check health coverage options

#

Marketplace and public coverage options depend on your household and where you live.

First step

Start at HealthCare.gov, or its Spanish site, to see your current options and enrollment steps.

Source

  • HealthCare.gov: Get coverage
05

Find mental health or substance use care

#

You do not have to find a provider by word of mouth alone.

First step

Search SAMHSA’s FindTreatment.gov by location; for an immediate emotional crisis, call or text 988.

Sources

  • SAMHSA: Treatment locators
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
06

Ask for rent help early

#

Programs and eligibility vary by state and locality.

First step

Call 211 or use USAGov’s rent-help page to find current local programs. Contact your landlord about options too.

Sources

  • USAGov: Emergency rent assistance
  • United Way 211: Local help
07

Check unemployment benefits after job loss

#

Each state runs its own program and sets its own rules.

First step

Use USAGov’s state finder to learn where and how to apply; keep records of your work and separation.

Source

  • USAGov: Unemployment benefits
08

Check a training program before enrolling

#

A costly certificate is useful only if it fits your target job and meets any licensing rules.

First step

Ask the school for total cost, completion and job outcomes, and accreditation. Confirm licensing requirements with your state board and compare other programs.

Source

  • FTC: Choosing a vocational school or certificate program
09

Check aid before paying for school

#

The FAFSA is the starting point for federal student aid and may also be used for state or school aid.

First step

If you plan to attend an eligible college or career school, use the official FAFSA site for the correct school year. Check your state and school deadlines separately.

Source

  • Federal Student Aid: Filling out the FAFSA form
10

Get advice for a housing problem

#

HUD-approved counselors can help with rental issues and housing plans; eviction counseling is free.

First step

Search for a HUD-approved agency or call 800-569-4287. Ask for help in your language. Eviction counseling is free; ask about fees for other services.

Source

  • HUD: About housing counseling
11

Check local housing voucher waitlists

#

Housing Choice Vouchers are handled by local public housing agencies, and openings and eligibility vary.

First step

Find your local public housing agency, ask if its voucher waitlist is open, and check income and documentation rules. Expect that a waitlist may be long.

Source

  • HUD: Housing Choice Voucher program
