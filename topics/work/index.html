A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR LIFE IN THE U.S.

Work and job-search steps in the U.S.

When work changes or something goes wrong on the job, write down the facts and find the right official starting point. These steps cover pay, safety, leave, job searches, and benefits; rules can depend on your job and state.

This page offers general information. Services and rules can vary by state and change over time. Check the sources and ask a qualified professional about your own medical, legal, or financial situation.

11 STEPS

Steps you can take

11 steps to explore. Choose one that fits your situation; each step has a source you can check.

01

Keep your own record of hours and pay

#

Clear records help when a paycheck or schedule is disputed.

First step

Save pay stubs and note shifts, hours, and messages about pay in a place you control.

Source

  • U.S. Department of Labor: Wage complaint
02

Ask about unpaid wages

#

The U.S. Department of Labor takes confidential wage complaints.

First step

Gather pay stubs, hours, and employer details; call 1-866-487-9243 or use the Wage and Hour Division site.

Source

  • U.S. Department of Labor: Wage complaint
03

Report a serious workplace hazard

#

Workers can ask OSHA to review unsafe or unhealthy conditions.

First step

Document the hazard and contact OSHA or your state plan; for imminent danger, use the emergency contact on OSHA’s page.

Source

  • OSHA: File a workplace safety complaint
04

Use a free job center

#

American Job Centers offer job-search help, workshops, and computer access.

First step

Search by ZIP code for a nearby center and ask which services are available in your language.

Source

  • U.S. Department of Labor: American Job Centers
05

Check your family or medical leave options

#

Federal and state protections may apply to a health or caregiving need.

First step

Read the Labor Department’s FMLA guide and ask your employer or state labor office about your situation.

Source

  • U.S. Department of Labor: Family and Medical Leave Act
06

Do not pay to get a job

#

A request for money or a check that you must forward is a common job-scam sign.

First step

Verify the employer through its own site; do not send money or sensitive data to a surprise recruiter.

Source

  • FTC: Job scams
07

Take work fatigue seriously

#

Fatigue can slow attention and judgment, especially with long or night shifts.

First step

Notice when you are too tired for a safety-critical task or drive; discuss breaks or a safer schedule with a supervisor when possible.

Source

  • CDC NIOSH: Fatigue and work
08

Compare benefits before changing jobs

#

Health coverage dates, premiums, doctors, and retirement vesting can change with a job move.

First step

Ask both employers for plan details. Write down when current coverage ends, when new coverage starts, and what happens to your retirement account.

Source

  • U.S. Department of Labor: Changing Jobs and Job Loss
09

Act quickly if job health coverage ends

#

Losing job-based insurance can open a limited window to enroll in a Marketplace plan; COBRA may also be an option.

First step

Check your Marketplace options promptly, usually within 60 days of losing coverage. Compare premiums, start dates, and any COBRA offer before choosing.

Source

  • HealthCare.gov: If you lose job-based coverage
10

Compare two careers before retraining

#

Pay, openings, entry requirements, and licenses differ by occupation and state.

First step

Use CareerOneStop’s Occupation Profile for two jobs in your area. Note typical wages, required training, and any state license before paying for a program.

Source

  • CareerOneStop: Occupation Profile
11

Look for a paid apprenticeship

#

Registered apprenticeships combine paid work, training, and a recognized credential.

First step

Search Apprenticeship.gov by field and location, then check each employer’s or sponsor’s application requirements.

Source

  • U.S. Department of Labor: Apprenticeship for career seekers
