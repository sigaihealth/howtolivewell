A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR LIFE IN THE U.S.

Practical health steps for life in the U.S.

A useful health plan can begin with one manageable action: a routine you can keep, a question for a clinician, or a preventive service to discuss. The steps below point to U.S. sources; personal care decisions belong with your health professional.

This page offers general information. Services and rules can vary by state and change over time. Check the sources and ask a qualified professional about your own medical, legal, or financial situation.

08 STEPS

Steps you can take

8 steps to explore. Choose one that fits your situation; each step has a source you can check.

01

Start with movement you can do

#

Some activity is better than none; the goal can grow with you.

First step

Walk, roll, dance, or do another accessible activity for a few minutes. Build toward 150 minutes of moderate activity a week and strength work twice a week.

Source

  • HHS: Physical Activity Guidelines
02

Protect a regular sleep window

#

Most adults need at least seven hours of sleep.

First step

Choose a wake time you can usually keep, then leave enough time for sleep. Ask a clinician if poor sleep persists.

Source

  • CDC: About sleep
03

Plan one affordable meal

#

Healthy food can include frozen, canned, and shelf-stable ingredients.

First step

Check what you already have, then plan one meal with vegetables or fruit, a protein, and a grain you like.

Source

  • CDC: Eating healthy on a budget
04

Swap one sugary drink

#

Sweetened drinks can add a lot of sugar without much nutrition.

First step

Try water, unsweetened tea, or sparkling water for one drink you usually buy.

Source

  • CDC: Rethink your drink
05

Know your blood pressure

#

High blood pressure often has no warning signs.

First step

Get a reading at a clinic, pharmacy, or with a home monitor. Share repeated high readings with your care team.

Source

  • CDC: Measuring your blood pressure
06

Make a personal screening plan

#

Useful tests depend on age, history, and other factors.

First step

Use MyHealthfinder, then ask a clinician which screenings make sense for you and how to get the results.

Source

  • HHS MyHealthfinder: Get screened
07

Review your vaccines

#

Recommendations change with age, health conditions, and new guidance.

First step

Bring your vaccine record to a pharmacist or clinician and ask what is recommended for you now.

Source

  • CDC: Vaccines recommended for adults
08

Get help to quit tobacco

#

Free quitline coaching can make quitting more manageable.

First step

Call 1-800-QUIT-NOW. For Spanish coaching, call 1-855-DÉJELO-YA.

Source

  • CDC: Free tobacco quitline support
