A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR LIFE IN THE U.S.

Everyday rights and options when something goes wrong

When a purchase, service, bill, or housing problem feels unfair, keep records and check the specific rule or complaint path before acting. These steps give starting points, while legal rights and deadlines depend on the facts and your location.

This page offers general information. Services and rules can vary by state and change over time. Check the sources and ask a qualified professional about your own medical, legal, or financial situation.

12 STEPS

Steps you can take

12 steps to explore. Choose one that fits your situation; each step has a source you can check.

01

Use the right office to replace an ID

#

Birth certificates, state IDs, and passports come from different agencies, with different requirements.

First step

Use USAGov’s document guide to find the issuing office before paying a third-party site. Store the replacement instructions with your important records.

Source

  • USAGov: Replace ID documents
02

Replace a Social Security card for free

#

You often need only your number, but a replacement card has no SSA fee if you do need one.

First step

Start at SSA.gov/number-card. Answer its questions to learn whether to apply online or make an office appointment; avoid sites charging for the form.

Source

  • Social Security: Replace a card
03

Update your address after a move

#

Forwarding mail does not automatically update every government account.

First step

Use the official USPS move page or a post office for forwarding. Then check which agencies, such as your state motor vehicle office, need a separate address change.

Source

  • USAGov: Change your address
04

Check your voter registration if eligible

#

Registration rules, deadlines, and address records differ by state.

First step

Choose your state at Vote.gov to see how to register or verify your record and the current deadline. Update your record if you have moved or changed your name.

Source

  • Vote.gov: Register or check registration
05

Look for civil legal aid early

#

Local programs may help with housing, family, consumer, or other civil problems, often based on income.

First step

Use USAGov’s legal-aid directory or the Legal Services Corporation locator. Ask about eligibility, language help, and any deadline in your paperwork.

Source

  • USAGov: Find affordable legal aid
06

Get help when an eviction notice arrives

#

State and local rules differ, and waiting can narrow your options.

First step

Keep the notice and your lease. Use USAGov’s eviction guide to find local housing help or legal aid, and ask what response date applies to your case.

Sources

  • USAGov: Avoid eviction
  • USAGov: Find affordable legal aid
07

Read a debt collector’s validation notice

#

It should help you identify the debt, amount, and how to dispute an error.

First step

Compare the notice with your records and note its dispute deadline. If something is wrong, use the CFPB guide to respond in writing and keep a copy.

Source

  • CFPB: Debt validation notices
08

Dispute a credit-report error

#

An inaccurate account or balance can affect access to credit.

First step

Tell both the credit reporting company and the business that supplied the data what is wrong. Include copies of supporting records and keep a copy of each dispute.

Source

  • CFPB: Dispute a credit-report error
09

Ask about hospital financial assistance

#

If a medical bill is hard to pay, you may qualify for help under the hospital’s policy.

First step

Ask billing for its financial assistance or charity-care policy, eligibility rules, and application deadline. Check the application status after you submit it.

Source

  • CMS: Medical bill financial assistance
10

Check an unexpected medical bill

#

Some out-of-network bills and some self-pay bills above a written estimate may qualify for federal protections.

First step

Keep the bill, insurance notice, and any estimate. Use CMS’s medical-bill guide to see which complaint or dispute process, if any, fits your situation.

Source

  • CMS: Medical bill rights
11

Ask about housing discrimination

#

HUD accepts reports about possible discrimination in renting, buying, and other housing activities; time limits can apply.

First step

Write down what happened, when, where, and who was involved. Contact HUD’s fair-housing office promptly to ask how to report it.

Source

  • HUD: Report housing discrimination
12

Take an unresolved purchase problem to the right office

#

State consumer protection offices can help route complaints about ordinary product and service disputes.

First step

For a business you know is legitimate, save receipts and messages and ask for a written resolution. If the issue remains, use USAGov’s state directory to find the right office.

Source

  • USAGov: State consumer protection offices
