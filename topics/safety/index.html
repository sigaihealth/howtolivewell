A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR LIFE IN THE U.S.

Everyday safety and emergency steps in the U.S.

When someone faces immediate danger, call 911. For other safety concerns, start with one step you can use or prepare now, then check the linked source for details that fit your situation.

This page offers general information. Services and rules can vary by state and change over time. Check the sources and ask a qualified professional about your own medical, legal, or financial situation.

07 STEPS

Steps you can take

7 steps to explore. Choose one that fits your situation; each step has a source you can check.

01

Know when to call 911

#

Emergency responders can act when someone faces immediate danger.

First step

For a life-threatening medical emergency, fire, or immediate threat, call 911 and say where you are.

Source

  • 911.gov: Calling 911
02

Save 988 for a hard moment

#

You can reach a crisis counselor for emotional distress, even if you are not thinking about suicide.

First step

Call or text 988. For Spanish, call 988 and press 2, or text AYUDA to 988. If someone is in immediate physical danger, call 911.

Source

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
03

Buckle up in every seat

#

A short trip or back seat still calls for a seat belt.

First step

Before the car moves, check that every passenger is buckled, including in a taxi or rideshare.

Source

  • NHTSA: Seat belt safety
04

Check a child’s car seat

#

The right restraint depends on the child’s size and the seat’s limits.

First step

Read the seat and vehicle instructions; use NHTSA’s guide and look for a free inspection if you are unsure.

Source

  • NHTSA: Car seats and booster seats
05

Prepare for an opioid overdose

#

Naloxone can reverse an opioid overdose while emergency help is on the way.

First step

If someone may be at risk, learn where to get naloxone. If you suspect an overdose, give it if available and call 911.

Source

  • CDC: Responding to an overdose
06

Save Poison Help

#

A poison specialist can guide you before symptoms appear.

First step

Save 1-800-222-1222. If someone collapses, has a seizure, cannot breathe, or cannot be awakened, call 911.

Source

  • America’s Poison Centers: Poison first aid
07

Get private help with relationship abuse

#

A trained advocate can help you think through safer options.

First step

Call 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788 when it is safe to do so. If you face immediate danger, call 911.

Source

  • National Domestic Violence Hotline
