A PRACTICAL GUIDE FOR LIFE IN THE U.S.

Build social connection and local support

Connection can begin with one reachable person or place, not a large social plan. Use these steps to check in, ask for support, or find a community setting that works for you.

This page offers general information. Services and rules can vary by state and change over time. Check the sources and ask a qualified professional about your own medical, legal, or financial situation.

On this page

  2. Put connection on the calendar

07 STEPS

Steps you can take

7 steps to explore. Choose one that fits your situation; each step has a source you can check.

01

Check in with one person

#

Small, genuine contact can help build supportive ties.

First step

Send a message that names something real: “I thought of you today. How are things?”

Source

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
02

Put connection on the calendar

#

Regular contact is easier to keep when it has a place in your week.

First step

Choose a short weekly call, walk, meal, or video chat with someone you want to know better.

Source

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
03

Join a group around an interest

#

Shared activities can create a sense of belonging.

First step

Look for a library, recreation, faith, volunteer, or neighborhood group that welcomes new people.

Source

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
04

Share an ordinary activity

#

Connection does not need an expensive or formal plan.

First step

Invite someone to cook, walk, shop, garden, or run an errand together.

Source

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
05

Ask for one specific kind of help

#

A clear request can make it easier for someone to support you.

First step

Try: “Could you call me tonight?” or “Could you give me a ride to this appointment?”

Source

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
06

Offer practical help without pressure

#

Support works best when it respects what the other person wants.

First step

Ask: “Would a meal, a ride, or company help this week?” Accept their answer.

Source

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
07

Get support if loneliness is weighing on you

#

Persistent disconnection can be hard to solve alone.

First step

Tell a trusted person or health professional what has been going on; ask about groups or care nearby.

Sources

  • CDC: Improving social connectedness
  • SAMHSA: Treatment locators
